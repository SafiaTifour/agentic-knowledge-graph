Bletchley Park is an English country house and estate in Bletchley, Milton Keynes, that became the principal centre of Allied code-breaking during the Second World War. During the conflict, the estate housed the Government Code and Cypher School, which regularly penetrated the secret communications of the Axis Powers—most importantly the German Enigma and Lorenz ciphers.

The nature of the work at Bletchley remained secret until many years after the war. According to the official historian of British Intelligence, the intelligence produced at Bletchley shortened the war by two to four years, and without it the outcome of the war would have been uncertain. The team of codebreakers included influential figures such as Alan Turing, Gordon Welchman, and Hugh Alexander.

The site was chosen for its location at the junction of the "Varsity Line" railway, which connected the universities of Oxford and Cambridge. These universities supplied many of the mathematicians and linguists who worked as codebreakers. By late 1944, the staff had grown to nearly 9,000 people working in three shifts around the clock to process the massive volume of intercepted messages.
